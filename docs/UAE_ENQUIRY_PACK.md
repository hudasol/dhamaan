# UAE enquiry pack: which regime covers an indoor service robot?

**Status: draft for the author to review and send. Nothing has been sent.** This pack makes no claim that any person or body has been contacted, has agreed to answer, or endorses this work. It is research support, not legal advice.

## 1. Why ask

The sources retrieved for this study cover drones (GCAA rules, Cabinet Resolution 110 of 2023) and road autonomous vehicles (Dubai Law 9 of 2023 and its executive rules, an Abu Dhabi permit scheme). No instrument specific to an indoor ground service robot was found (assurance case assumption AS4; table rows C47 to C56, C64 to C66, D50, D51). That is a statement about what was searched and retrieved, not about the law. The question can only be settled by the bodies concerned.

## 2. Who to ask (candidates to confirm, no contact is claimed)

| Candidate | Why it might be relevant | Basis in this study |
|---|---|---|
| The federal ministry or body responsible for industrial product conformity and standards | A robot is a manufactured product. A drone product status process is run by a ministry (MOIAT) and appears in a consultation page. | C63 (summary seen). Whether it covers ground robots is not known. |
| The emirate level authority responsible for the building or the free zone where the host organisation sits | Local rules may cover robots operating in public or semi public indoor spaces. | No row. The authority depends on the emirate and has to be confirmed. |
| The general civil aviation authority | To confirm the answer is "outside our scope" for a ground robot with no flight function. | C47 to C58 cover unmanned aircraft only. |
| The host organisation (health and safety office, estates or facilities, legal or compliance office, insurer contact) | The deploying organisation sets site rules and insurance conditions even where no law is specific. | None. Enquiry B below. |

Before sending, confirm the correct recipient and channel from the authority's own website. Do not guess addresses.

## 3. Enquiry A: to the UAE authority (draft)

> **Subject:** Which regime applies to an indoor autonomous service robot? (information request)
>
> Dear Sir or Madam,
>
> I am [YOUR NAME], [ROLE, for example student researcher] at [ORGANISATION]. I am studying safety evidence for autonomous service robots and would be grateful for general guidance on one point.
>
> **Scenario.** A wheeled mobile robot, with no flight function, operates indoors in a [library / public building] in [EMIRATE]. It moves among staff and visitors, including children and people with disabilities, and uses camera and lidar based perception, some of it learning based. It may later carry a small manipulator arm. It is not a vehicle for the public road. It is not sold in this scenario; it is deployed by the building operator.
>
> **Questions.**
> 1. Which UAE federal or [EMIRATE] regime, if any, governs the safe deployment of such a robot (product safety, workplace safety, public space operation, data or other)?
> 2. Which body is responsible, and is any registration, permit, conformity step or notification needed before use?
> 3. Do you refer to any standards, for example for machinery or service robot safety, or to any technical regulation?
> 4. If this robot falls outside your remit, can you point me to the body that covers it?
>
> I would be glad of a short reply or a pointer to the relevant public document. I will treat any answer as general information and will cite it as such. I am not asking for approval of any design.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> [YOUR NAME] | [ROLE] | [CONTACT DETAILS]

## 6. What to do with an answer

- If a body names a regime or instrument: add a row to the evidence table with the document as source, and read the document before recording a clause. Update assurance item EV-03 (`assurance/library_robot_case.yaml`).
- If a body says the robot is outside its remit: record that as the answer, with date and sender, and keep EV-03 open for the other candidates.
- No answer is itself information, but is not evidence that no rule exists.
