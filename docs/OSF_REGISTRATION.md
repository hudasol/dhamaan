## Study information

Title: Do synthetic fault generators and recorded GNSS interference give the same verdicts for the same integrity monitor? Round 3 of the dhamaan study.

Description and hypotheses (H3 to H5 of `docs/RESEARCH_QUESTION.md`):

| Id | Hypothesis | Data and test | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|---|
| H3 | At least one acceptance verdict for the GNSS monitor differs between the synthetic arm and the recorded arm. | Round 3: same monitor, same thresholds, recorded logs with known intervals against the synthetic generator. | No verdict differs; the synthetic arm is then adequate for this monitor. |
| H4 | On recorded data the monitor detects later and raises more false alarms, because recorded errors are correlated in time and heavy tailed. | Detection delay and false alarm rate by arm. | Equal or opposite direction. |
| H5 | A generator recalibrated on a training set of recordings reproduces the verdicts on held out recordings; the original generator does not. | Split recordings before looking at results; calibrate on one part, score on the other. | The recalibrated generator still disagrees on held out data: synthetic faults cannot stand in for this claim. |

## Design plan

Study type: computational, replay of existing data. Blinding: none; scripts score. Randomisation: a seeded split of recordings, drawn before any metric is computed.

## 1. Parts

| Part | Question | New runs |

## Analysis plan

## 2. Part A: informativeness of predictions

A prediction is informative if its verdict changes under at least one mutant. Mutants, each applied to the whole model and rerun on the same seeds:

- M0: a monitor that never alarms.
- M1: a monitor that alarms at every fix.
- M2: each assumed parameter multiplied by 0.5 and by 1.5, one at a time (parameters listed from the code by a script, not by hand).
- M3: the fault onset time shifted by a fixed offset.

Output: for every prediction, the verdict under the original model and under each mutant; the count of predictions whose verdict never changes; the hit rate among informative predictions only. Reported next to the headline hit rates, which stay as published.

Prediction (written now): at least a quarter of the 42 predictions are unchanged under every mutant.

## 3. Part B: sensitivity

For each acceptance criterion of both rounds, the verdict under each parameter change in M2. Output: a table of criteria against parameters, marking each flip, and a list of the criteria that never flip and the ones that flip under a change of 50 percent or less.

Prediction (written now): at least one criterion that held in rounds 1 or 2 flips under a change of 50 percent or less in one assumed parameter.

## 5. Part D: independently written generator
